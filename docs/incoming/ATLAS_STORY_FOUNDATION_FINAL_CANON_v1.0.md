## 0. Priority and scope

This document is the authoritative, concise story-foundation lock for Atlas. Keep `Atlas_of_the_Lonely_World_UNIFIED_PROJECT_SOURCE_v1.8.md` as the broader active source for gameplay, visuals, planning, and detailed decision registers. Where older proposals or archived files conflict with this foundation, this document and later **explicit** Game Director decisions prevail. Never silently promote `PROPOSAL`, `OPEN`, or `LEGACY` details to canon.

The locked macro-chronology is: natural Resonance and Origin Core → Golem civilization → coexistence and collaboration with humanity → development and governance of the Seven Spires → ideological fracture (Freedom vs Stewardship) → war and cascading resonance disruption → destabilized Origin Core and fractured world → cooperative rescue and emergency Spire stabilization (The Last Separation) → Age of Silence and Nomad isolation → Okto's journey → discoveries, understanding, and future decisions across Games 1–3.

## 1. Cosmology — locked foundation

1. **Resonance is natural**, predating Golems, humanity, and the Spires. It mediates alignment among systems, not unlimited free energy or all-purpose magic.
2. **Origin Core is a natural world-scale phenomenon/structure** associated with resonant balance. Golems did not create it. Its physical manifestation is not locked.
3. Resonant imbalance can propagate; restoration and stabilization have limited capacity, require support, and involve meaningful trade-offs.
4. Golems have their own individual resonance patterns related to embodiment and consciousness. They are autonomous living beings, not machines or a hive mind.
5. Certain materials and structures preserve limited resonance traces, not flawless historical recordings.

## 2. The ancient civilizations and Seven Spires

1. **Golem civilization predates humanity.** The details of Golem origins and reproduction remain open.
2. Humans and Golems coexisted, studied one another, and collaborated over a long period. Neither species is intrinsically the villain.
3. The **Seven Spires** were originally civilization-enabling infrastructure for the regulated use of resonance and interregional connection. They were **not originally essential to keeping an undamaged world intact**.
4. A shared arrangement governing access and safety existed, referred to in working history as **the Covenant**. The exact formal name and legal structure are not locked.
5. Following the war, surviving Spires were repurposed into emergency infrastructure regulating, connecting, and anchoring/stabilizing the fragmented world's surviving regions. Exact functions of each Spire are open.

## 3. The Broken Covenant and world fracture

1. The central ideological dispute was **Freedom versus Stewardship**, not a simple humans-versus-Golems racial war. Each side included both species and held understandable principles as well as dangerous extremes.
2. Freedom advocated fair, transparent, broader access and opposed monopoly; its extreme could disregard safeguards. Stewardship advocated risk governance and long-term stability; its extreme could impose coercive restrictions.
3. Conflict escalated into a great war. The broad W2 → W3 → W1 escalation direction was approved earlier, **but the specific incidents, culprits, names, dates, and event details are not final canon**.
4. Destructive resonant disruption during the war produced a cascading disturbance of Origin Core, destabilizing and fragmenting the world. The rescue teams did **not** cause the original fracture.
5. **The Last Separation** was a multi-participant, cross-species rescue effort. Survivors reconfigured remaining Spires to stabilize what could be saved. No single savior did all the work.
6. The aftermath brought loss of knowledge, a decline in Golem civilization, altered human communities, and the **Age of Silence**. The fate and distribution of remaining Golems are unresolved.

## 4. Oru, Moss, and the mother

1. Stone Cub is an ancient, conscious, autonomous young Golem, later named **Moss** by Okto.
2. His original name is **Oru**, given by his mother, meaning **“a small cherished life” / “kehidupan kecil yang disayangi.”** The name signifies maternal affection, not prophecy, a code, or a Spire activation key.
3. Oru's mother was one of the specialists/collaborators in the ancient rescue. She tried to save her child, not turn him into a battery, seal, sacrifice, or piece of machinery.
4. A **Resonance Imprint formed accidentally** during that rescue. Its exact capabilities and implications remain open.
5. Oru survived through dormancy and has fragmented memories, not perfect access to ancient history.
6. **Moss and Oru are the same individual**; neither name invalidates the other. The original name **Oru is revealed in Game 2**, through exploration and traces connected to his mother. The exact reveal scene remains unapproved.
7. Moss's later **Resonance Instability** arises at the direction level from post-dormancy vulnerability interacting with the changed world. He retains agency. Spire support is limited and does not function as a magical cure. Precise medical/technical mechanics remain open.

## 5. Nomad Valley and Okto

1. The Nomad community has been **isolated for seven generations due to the consequences of the ancient war and destruction outside the valley**. Nomads no longer know the true reason. Do not introduce a separate unrelated local disaster as the primary cause.
2. Seven generations of isolation **does not itself establish that the war occurred exactly seven generations ago**. Absolute chronology and the point when isolation began are open.
3. **Okto** is the protagonist's personal name; **the Cartographer** describes his role. He is a young Nomad who inherits his father's unfinished map.
4. Okto chooses to leave the small known world and explore/map the unknown outside. The precise inciting incident and route of escape remain to be designed.
5. Okto meets Stone Cub in the outside world (the current Game 1 route places this around **Broken Crossing**); Okto gives him the name Moss. Their companionship grows through two-way help, nonverbal communication, trust, curiosity, and vulnerability—not by treating Moss as a puzzle tool.
6. The Living Map connects player exploration to the story of discovery and growing responsibility. Environmental storytelling reveals history gradually without exhaustive exposition.

## 6. Three-game narrative contract

### Game 1 — DISCOVER

Okto leaves Nomad Valley with his father's unfinished map, discovers a beautiful but fractured ancient world, and befriends Moss. Through travel and collaboration they encounter evidence of the old human–Golem civilization. Moss's resonant condition deteriorates, directing them toward the **Continental Spire**. Local story closure and Okto's moral choice must be satisfying **within Game 1**, not postponed to Game 2.

**Climactic conflict: The Weight of One Life.** A limited Spire capacity creates a fair, explained trade-off between helping Moss and maintaining local infrastructure:

- **Preserve the Balance:** maintain regional stabilization and connection. Moss survives and receives limited help, but is not fully recovered.
- **The Quiet Promise:** allocate some limited capacity to help stabilize Moss more substantially, though not cure him. One regional connecting route is closed temporarily and in a controlled way, with a safe alternative and meaningful social/travel impact.

Moss must have agency, the consequences must be explained before the player chooses, neither ending is secretly the single correct choice, and neither causes automatic mass death. Both receive meaningful epilogues. Fine implementation and individual scenes remain open.

### Game 2 — UNDERSTAND

The journey expands beyond Game 1's local geography and uncovers more of the fractured world, the network of Spires, human–Golem relations, and the war. **Oru's original name and the emotional significance of his mother are revealed in Game 2**, not Game 1. The narrative respects the consequences of both Game 1 endings rather than negating them. Exact destinations and plot turns remain open.

### Game 3 — DECIDE

The trilogy confronts the long-term consequences for Origin Core, the Spires, and the future of the world and its peoples. The final solution, political outcome, physical fate of the world, and character fates have **not** been locked. A local Game 1 choice does not already repair the world-scale Core.

## 7. Narrative and visual principles

- Maintain three pillars together: **meaningful exploration, engaging traversal/puzzles, and the emotional journey of Okto and Moss**.
- The world is **living, ancient, monumental, and beautiful**, despite historic tragedy. It must not become a uniformly dead or bleak wasteland.
- Ruins and vast silhouettes communicate the world's history and scale, but moodboard images are **visual reference, not proof of exact lore**. A Golem-shaped mountain does not automatically canonize a living giant or a fossil.
- Favor environmental evidence, gesture, sound, resonance, and player action over exposition dumps. Places may acquire new meaning as the player learns their history.
- Preserve Moss's agency, personhood, and the mother's care. Never revive the legacy "Cub battery / seventh seal" or "Origin Core is a prison for certain evil" stories without a new director decision.

## 8. Explicitly still OPEN — do not invent

1. Absolute dates, exact war trigger, perpetrators, detailed political factions, the formal name of the Covenant, and exact fracture physics.
2. Origin Core physical form; mechanics of floating lands and Sea of Clouds; separate names, placement, and operating conditions of all Seven Spires.
3. Golem origin, reproduction, surviving population, and detailed causal breakdown of its decline.
4. Mother's name, specific actions and fate; details of Oru's accidental imprint and memory recovery.
5. How stabilization of Moss works technically, exact illness symptoms, and exact interactive ending sequence.
6. Precise Game 2/3 scenes, branch continuity, ending resolution, and any legacy prophecy/ancient monster premise.

## 9. Development governance

**Canonical reference order:** (1) explicit latest Game Director decision; (2) this FINAL STORY FOUNDATION v1.0 for the foundational history and trilogy; (3) Unified Project Source v1.8 for detailed approved decisions outside this narrow lock; (4) older canon drafts/review logs as historical evidence only.

Do not rewrite the foundations under the guise of elaboration. Any proposed major lore alteration requires a decision ID, explicit description of its effect on **Game 1, Game 2, and Game 3**, and Game Director approval before becoming canon. Production may proceed on approved foundations without waiting to finalize every open detail.

**One-sentence permanent premise:** In a world fractured by a war born from competing ideals of freedom and stewardship, a young cartographer from a community isolated by the catastrophe befriends a surviving child of the ancient Golem civilization; their journey gradually uncovers the past and leads toward choices about care, responsibility, and the future of their world.
