# ATLAS OF THE LONELY WORLD — OPENING GAMEPLAY BLUEPRINT A01–A07
Version 1.0 | 2026-11-01 | Basis: Master Canon Final v4.0 + 45 keputusan Game Director

## Authority and scope
This document specifies the player experience from New Game through the R02→R03 boundary. The Game Director's 45 decisions are locked design direction; concrete choreography, hazard layouts, timings, and interaction mechanisms below are production proposals requiring greybox validation, not additional canon revelations. Do not reveal Oru, Vara, Origin Core, or the full history of the Spires. Okto's father is deceased; his ancient map was found inside Nomad Valley and is newly discovered by Okto during the prologue. Moss first appears in A05, never earlier.

## Creative contract
Experience arc: belonging → curiosity → commitment → awe → solitude → surprise → cautious cooperation → emerging companionship. Side-on 2D painterly traversal, medium platforming difficulty, nonmagical contextual Survey, Living Map documenting observed/traversed locations, no combat/dash/double-jump/wall-jump. No permanent HUD except map; transient contextual prompts and optional delayed hint text are permitted. Cinematics may be more frequent at emotional beats but must be short, avoid taking away control mid-challenge, and hand control back quickly.

## Screen-to-screen sequence
### Main menu / New Game
Animated, inhabited Nomad Valley panorama, gentle piano with ambient wind/birds and exploratory motif. Menu controls: Continue (when save exists), New Game, Settings, Quit where supported; clear focus state and input navigation. New Game fades directly into the same valley from a coherent camera angle. No lore exposition or prophetic framing.

### Opening cinematic — target 90–120 seconds, to test
1. Wide sunrise: layered farms, smoke from chimneys, sparse purposeful activity; quiet rather than empty.
2. Camera approaches Okto's house and father's unused workspace; absent presence through tools, dust, and untouched objects, not explicit death exposition.
3. Okto finds the ancient unfinished map for the first time, unfolds it, sees recognizable valley contours and an incomplete line outside its edge; short close-up and facial/body reaction.
4. Minimal poetic on-screen text may establish perspective, but must not claim the map predicts the future or that the father left the valley. Proposed placeholder line: “Ada tempat yang belum pernah kulihat.” This is optional copy pending narrative polish.
5. Okto finishes preparing and takes position by the exit. Seamless camera handoff to gameplay. No mandatory house exploration or supply gathering.

### A01 — Nomad Settlement
Primary path: house door → quiet residential passage → working terrace → outer stone path → ridge approach. Two very short optional viewing pockets rejoin the main route; no village quest hub. Contextual movement prompts for move, run, jump at natural low-risk obstacles; prompts disappear once used. A few citizens work, repair tools, tend plants, or pause and look; Okto departs quietly without public farewell, though subtle reactions preserve the canon that some are concerned or supportive. A familiar small step, climbable ledge, and short run-up teach controls without a tutorial wall. Living Map available to inspect, showing the known valley base and unfinished external trace, not a completed world map. Exit threshold leads continuously toward A02.

### A02 — Survey Ridge
Topography: uphill terraces → stable stone ledges → eroded lookout → hidden fracture mouth. Challenge escalates gently: single jump, ledge grab, climb up, controlled descent; a visibly cracked old stone shelf shifts after landing, drops loose grit, then collapses only after a generous reaction interval, exposing a recoverable lower route. Avoid surprise instant death. Main observation puzzle: at a contextual Survey point, compare the map's incomplete symbol to a distinct rock formation and its visible alignment with an old cut-stone marker. Survey clarifies the observed alignment, not the solution; player chooses the route to the concealed gap. Wrong branch is short, provides a new view and loops back. From the lookout the valley remains visible; exterior world remains occluded by mountains. Checkpoint before the exposed traversal.

### A03 — Cliff Passage / First Horizon Reveal
Natural cleft with fragments of ancient stonework: narrow exterior ledge → hanging lip with ledge grab → controlled descent into shadow → short tight passage → outward opening. Movement feels more committed, not an extreme precision test. Hazards are physically telegraphed: gravel fall, creaking masonry, a shifting block. Recoverable falls where geological shelf exists; otherwise quick checkpoint. Sound narrows to footfall, wind, stone, and near-silence; no Moss cue. On exiting, camera widens to show the vast broken crossing and downstream water landscape, ambience thins, orchestral motif gradually swells. Return control immediately after framing. Player may linger, pan within camera constraints, or proceed; no mandatory button to 'appreciate' view. Mark the panorama in Living Map only once actually witnessed.

### A04 — Crossing Rim (solo)
First encounter with R02: immense damaged ancient bridgework, wet cliffs, flowing water, green regrowth, birds/insects and visible ecology. Okto is alone for a meaningful gameplay stretch. One obvious primary traversal spine with two short optional loops: (1) elevated lookout / sketch of span geometry; (2) lower sheltered ruins / material history and fauna. Both reconnect, neither is mandatory for progression or ending. The map records travel but is not necessary to solve navigation here. Primary challenges: broken bridge segment, ledge shimmy, stepped descent, recoverable lower ledge; cracked masonry warns before yielding. An optional falling slab creates a new shortcut rather than simply punishing the player. At the far end the level funnels to A05 via an unstable but legible old span. Avoid showing Moss before A05.

### A05 — Fallen Span / first encounter
Okto crosses an old shelf; cracks, sound and dust provide warning. A partial collapse drops him onto a safe lower chamber shelf, visibly cuts off the immediate return route, and creates a physical exit problem. He is not killed by a forced failure. Moss is in the same connected chamber for independent reasons; first appears as a small alert creature, approaches Okto out of curiosity, and startles him. Initial distance and wary gestures matter; no instant friendship and no identity reveal.
Puzzle sequence: (1) Okto reaches an upper brace via jump/climb and releases a jammed counterweight or support, creating an opening Moss can use; (2) Moss independently notices the new access, passes through a narrow opening, and shifts a small counterweight/latch from the far side, making Okto's exit climb possible; (3) as both traverse the weakened final platform, the platform gives way in a clearly telegraphed set piece; Okto secures a reachable support and creates a foothold for Moss, while Moss holds/repositions a small stabilizing element so Okto can finish crossing. The rescue is reciprocal and readable; Moss acts voluntarily, not as a remotely commanded key. Avoid making Moss an energy battery or explaining Resonance Imprint. If player stalls, animation/attention cues and eventually short hint text; no repeated companion command. Safe checkpoints at the start and after the first cooperative success. Camera never cuts away during a required timed input.

### A06 — Shared Bridgeworks
After escaping, Moss follows with hesitation, occasionally stops to study Okto, and gradually closes distance. A short contextual cooperative obstacle reinforces the earlier cause-and-effect: Okto moves a fallen timber or opens a route, Moss chooses to explore a narrow passage and shifts a latch; Okto responds by climbing through. Companion behavior states: cautious follow, investigate, wait at safe edge, contextual initiative, regroup, and safety teleport only offscreen as a last resort. Moss must not soft-lock the level or be left permanently behind. Short optional shared vista with idle animations and no dialogue; neither is forced to sit. One progressively more complex traversal passage follows; failure remains fair.

### A07 — Downstream Descent
Terrain follows water downhill into greener terraces: broken service stairs → wet stone shelves → sheltered descent → first view of Aqueduct greenery. Moss and Okto negotiate a final moderate traversal with companion pathfinding, not an obligatory second major puzzle. Environmental reaction: a waterlogged wooden walkway flexes and sheds debris before giving way, allowing a safe lower route if missed; water flow and rock strata visually justify elevation. A calm final pause by moving water lets the player see the pair together before the R03 transition. Do not introduce R03's full aqueduct mechanics prematurely. Record the crossing and observed landmark in Living Map.

## Reactive environment design system
Hazard contract: identifiable material weakness → pre-contact visual cue → contact reaction (sound, shift, dust) → sufficient response window → physically plausible state change → persistent or resettable outcome → recoverable route or nearby checkpoint. No randomized unavoidable collapse. Categories: brittle old stone shelves, loose scree, weathered bridge timber, aged metal supports, hanging masonry, wet roots, eroded stair edges. Not every object is destructible. Visual readability at actual Godot camera scale is mandatory. Audio must differentiate warning crack from background ambience. Persist major opened routes in the active level; reset short challenge props on checkpoint only where needed for solvability. Design a bypass/recovery if the player remains below after collapse.

## Interactions and feedback
Movement uses walk/run/jump/ledge grab/shimmy/climb/controlled descent. Survey only at designated meaningful observation nodes; no magical scanning. Living Map automatically adds visited paths and actually seen landmarks, not unseen secrets; sketch flourish only at key moments. No persistent HUD; contextual button prompt appears near a valid interaction and fades. After repeated failure or extended nonprogress, a short non-spoiling text hint may appear; give observational hints before explicit route hints. Menu/settings accessibility should offer readable subtitles/text, rebindable controls where practical, and options to reduce camera shake / flashing; settings scope is a production recommendation.

## Timing targets and validation
Nomad Valley A01–A03: ~10–12 minutes of gameplay plus short cinematic, as established in canon. A04–A07 durations are intentionally uncommitted until greybox; measure solo exploration, first encounter comprehension, companion regroup delays, and recovery-route time. Run tests for first-time players: controls readable without forced exposition; hidden exit discoverable with one observational puzzle; horizon emotional beat not interrupted; solo segment feels meaningfully solo; Moss contributes two-way actions; no unexpected instant-death collapse; optional branches return; player can complete A01–A07 without opening the map repeatedly.

## Godot implementation work packages
1. Character scale and camera calibration at target viewport; unified sprite master and baseline/pivot for all states.
2. Greybox of A01–A07 and camera zones, route visibility, lower-route recovery, collision, ledge handling, and checkpoint logic.
3. Reusable reactive prop state machine: Stable → Warning → Failing → Settled; signal-driven audio/VFX, collision updates, save state.
4. Contextual Survey node, map observation log, short prompts and delayed hints.
5. A05 staged encounter controller with soft-lock guards; Moss AI finite state machine and path anchors; offscreen recovery.
6. Playtest logs for time, deaths, missed jumps, missed landmark cues, camera readability, hint usage, Moss pathfinding stalls.
7. Only then produce final visual assets, animation and cinematics.

None of these proposals creates new historical revelations or rewrites Game 2–3.
